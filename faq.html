Getting started

What is EBiM-Lite?
EBiM-Lite is the dual SO-101 adaptation of the EBiM (Embodied Bimanual Manipulation) benchmark. It uses two SO-ARM101 arms on a TurtleBot 4 and adapts the three tracks of the full benchmark. See EBiM vs EBiM-Lite.
Who can participate?
University and industry learners. Detailed eligibility rules will be published in the rulebook.
Do I need my own robot?
No. Stage I runs in simulation (Isaac Sim / Isaac Lab). Selected Stage II teams get a DGX Spark or RTX machine on loan, and a limited number of SO-101 arms are provided.
Where can I learn the required skills?
See the recommended NVIDIA DLI courses in Learning & Compute.

The competition

Is teleoperation allowed?
No. Only full autonomy is evaluated.
How are ties broken?
Completion time is the tie-breaker in all tracks. In Track 3, ranking uses the highest completed stage first, then total score, then completion time.
Can my team enter more than one track?
Each track is ranked separately. Rules for entering several tracks will be published in the rulebook.
How do we submit?
The submission format will be announced before the simulation pre-selection (Feb 25, 2027). See Submission.
Where is the final?
At GTC Taipei in mid-May 2027. The top 3 teams receive prizes.

Compute

What compute do teams get?
Stage I: L40 120 hr, A100 100 hr, H100 50 hr, plus Brev credits. Stage II: an RTX / DGX Spark for 40 days plus the same GPU hours. See Computing resources.
How do we apply for compute?
The application process will be announced.
