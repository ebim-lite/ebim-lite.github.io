General rules

Same tasks, same rules, from simulation to the real robot

🤖

Full autonomy only

No teleoperation during evaluated runs. Policies must perceive, plan and act on their own.

🏁

3 tracks, ranked separately

Teams may enter one or more tracks. Each track has its own leaderboard and prizes.

🔁

Sim → Real

Isaac Sim pre-selection decides which teams advance to cross-site validation and the real-robot final.

Track 1Deformable Object Manipulation

Cable Routing

Industrial scenario: path recognition → cable grasping → routing → fixation, on a 20 cm × 20 cm board.

Scenario

  • Route the cable through the given fixture configuration, in the specified sequence and direction
  • Only full autonomy is evaluated: no teleoperation
  • 60-minute time limit per attempt
  • The task board is scaled down from the full EBiM version to fit the SO-101 workspace

Scoring

Completion Rate = Prefix ÷ Total Prefix = number of consecutive correct fixtures counted from the start
  • Primary metric: completion rate (%)
  • Tie-breaker: completion time
A mistake breaks the prefix: fixtures routed correctly after the first error do not count.
Track 2Precision Placement

Thermal Pad Pick-and-Place

Industrial electronics manufacturing: a Thermal Interface Material (TIM) assembly benchmark.

Scenario

  • Pick a thermal pad and place it on a randomly assigned target
  • Only 3 memory modules are used as candidate targets
  • Keep the correct orientation: a wrong orientation scores 0
  • Placement accuracy is scored by IoU (0.0 – 1.0)

Scoring

Score = P × O × IoU Pick success (0/1) × Orientation correct (0/1) × IoU (0–1)
  • Primary metric: P × O × IoU
  • Secondary metric: raw IoU
  • Tie-breaker: completion time
Track 3Long-Horizon Embodied Manipulation

Service Robot Challenge

A full service cycle (table setup → bean recovery → cleanup) across the Kitchen, Dining and Garbage Collection areas, using a cup, a bowl of coffee beans and a spoon.

Points breakdown (max 10)

Setup · 3
Bean Recovery · 4
Cleanup · 3

Ranking priority

  1. Highest completed stage
  2. Total score
  3. Completion time
Example: a team that finishes Stage 2 ranks above a team that finishes only Stage 1, even if the second team has more total points.
Stage 1 · 3 pts

Table Setup

  • Transport the cup, the bowl (with coffee beans) and the spoon from the Kitchen to the Dining Area
  • Three randomly assigned targets: one for the cup, and spots next to the robot head for the bowl and spoon
  • Complete when every object sits at its assigned location
Stage 2 · 4 pts

Bean Recovery

  • Transfer the coffee beans from the bowl into the recycling container in the Garbage Collection Area
  • The container sits on a scale: Recovery Ratio = recovered mass ÷ original mass
  • Proportional points: 100% → full, 90% → 90%, 80% → 80%
Stage 3 · 3 pts

Cleanup

  • Return the cup, bowl and spoon to the Kitchen Area
  • Place each one inside the sink region, the black rectangle on the countertop
  • 1 point per object inside the sink region
Competition platform

Low-cost mobile bimanual robot

Two SO-ARM101 arms on a TurtleBot 4. Open-source hardware and software lower the barrier for university teams, and the compact footprint fits the 100 cm aisles and 30 cm tables of the EBiM-Lite arena.

Call for participation

Who can join and how

Stage I · Simulation

  • Open to all university and industry learners
  • Register your team by Dec 21, 2026
  • Complete the recommended DLI courses (see Learning & Compute)
  • Develop policies in the provided Isaac Lab task environments
  • Submit for simulation pre-selection: Feb 25 – Mar 25, 2027

Stage II · Real Robot

  • About 10 teams selected from the simulation results
  • Loaned DGX Spark / RTX workstation and SO-101 + TurtleBot 4 hardware
  • Offline preliminary rounds at regional validation sites
  • Finalists compete live at GTC Taipei, mid-May 2027
