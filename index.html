EBiM-Lite 2026: Low-Cost Bimanual Robots, Real-World Tasks

A competition that takes university teams from Isaac Sim to a real mobile bimanual robot. Train in simulation, validate on real robots across Taiwan, and compete in the real-robot final at GTC Taipei.

Overview

Two stages, with more resources at each stage

All teams start in simulation. Selected teams get hardware and compute to take their policies onto a real dual-arm robot. NVIDIA is the technical sponsor, supplying compute, learning content and arms.

I
Stage I · Simulation
Dec 2026 – Mar 2027 · All teams
  • NVIDIA robotics learning materials provided to all teams
  • Brev credits for learning and limited model training
  • Deterministic simulation tasks in Isaac Sim / Isaac Lab
  • Simulation pre-selection: Feb 25 – Mar 25, 2027
II
Stage II · Selected Teams
Mar – May 2027 · ~10 teams
  • DGX Spark or RTX machine lent to selected teams
  • Limited number of LeRobot SO-101 arms provided
  • Offline preliminary rounds at on-site validation sites
  • Additional GPU hours (L40 / A100 / H100)
★
Final · Real Robot
Mid-May 2027 · GTC Taipei
  • Full-autonomy evaluation in the real arena
  • Leaderboard and awards
  • Prizes for the top 3 teams
  • Workshop and benchmark report

Key themes

What EBiM-Lite measures

Sim-to-Real Transfer

Isaac Sim pre-selection, then the same tasks and scoring on the real robot.

Deformable & Precision Manipulation

Cable routing and thermal pad placement from industrial manufacturing.

Long-Horizon Mobile Manipulation

A full service cycle across kitchen, dining and garbage collection areas.

Accessible Hardware

Open-source, low-cost hardware and software lower the barrier for university teams.

How it works

Simulation → Cross-Site Validation → Real-World Evaluation

The same three tracks and scoring rules apply from simulation to the real final.

SimulationCompete virtually in Isaac Lab.
→
Cross-Site ValidationTaipei/Taoyuan · Tainan/Kaohsiung · Hsinchu (TBC)
→
Real-World FinalLive at GTC Taipei, followed by awards and the benchmark report.
Tracks

Three industrial and service scenarios

Each track is ranked separately. Only full autonomy is evaluated: no teleoperation.

Track 1 · Deformable Objects

Cable Routing

Route the cable through fixtures in the given configuration, sequence and direction.

Primary: Completion rate · Tie-break: time
Track 2 · Precision Placement

Thermal Pad Pick-and-Place

Pick a thermal pad and place it on a randomly assigned target with the correct orientation.

Score: P × O × IoU · Tie-break: time
Track 3 · Long-Horizon

Service Robot Challenge

Full service cycle: table setup → bean recovery → cleanup.

Score: max 10 pts · Stage → score → time
Platform

Two SO-ARM101 arms on a TurtleBot 4

A low-cost mobile bimanual robot. Open-source hardware and software lower the barrier for university teams.

  • Manipulation: two SO-ARM101 6-DoF arms with parallel grippers and wrist cameras
  • Mobile base: TurtleBot 4 (iRobot Create 3) with OAK-D camera and lidar
  • Compact footprint: suits the 100 cm aisles and 30 cm tables of the arena
