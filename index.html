Overview

Two stages, with more resources at each stage

All teams start in simulation. The top teams get real hardware and compute to take their policies onto a physical dual-arm robot. We supply compute, learning content and robot arms. The academic host runs the event.

I
Stage I · Simulation
Dec 2026 – Mar 2027 · All teams
  • Robotics learning materials (DLI courses) for every team
  • Cloud credits for learning and limited model training
  • Deterministic simulation tasks in Isaac Sim / Isaac Lab
  • Simulation pre-selection: Feb 25 – Mar 25, 2027
II
Stage II · Selected Teams
Mar – May 2027 · ~10 teams
  • DGX Spark or RTX workstation lent for 40 days
  • SO-101 arms and TurtleBot 4 base provided (limited quantity)
  • Offline preliminary rounds at regional validation sites
  • Additional GPU hours for sim-to-real training
★
Final · Real Robot
Mid-May 2027 · GTC Taipei

  • Live leaderboard and awards ceremony

Tracks

Three industrial and service scenarios

Each track is ranked separately. Only full autonomy is evaluated: no teleoperation.

Track 1 · Deformable Objects

Cable Routing

Recognize the path, grasp the cable and route it through fixtures in the given order and direction on a 20 × 20 cm board.

Primary: Completion rate · Tie-break: time
Track 2 · Precision Placement

Thermal Pad Pick-and-Place

Pick a thermal pad (TIM) and place it on a randomly assigned memory module with the correct orientation.

Score: P × O × IoU · Tie-break: time
Track 3 · Long-Horizon

Service Robot Challenge

A full service cycle: set the table, recover the coffee beans, then clean up into the sink.

Score: max 10 pts · Stage → score → time
Platform

Two SO-ARM101 arms on a TurtleBot 4

A low-cost mobile bimanual robot built from open-source hardware and software, so any university team can take part.

  • Manipulation: 2 × SO-ARM101, 6-DoF, parallel grippers and wrist cameras
  • Mobile base: TurtleBot 4 (iRobot Create 3), OAK-D camera and lidar
  • Compact arena: 100 cm aisles, 30 cm-high round tables (Ø 70 cm)
  • Open source: LeRobot-compatible arms and an open software stack
